# Evidence ledger — first memory and learning slice

**Scope:** Only the initial educational memory/learning slice. This is not a complete review of cognitive training, a clinical guideline, or a claim that the app's activity is validated.  
**Review date:** 2026-10-04. **Reviewer:** coordinating coding agent in the evidence-reviewer role; independent subject-matter review remains outstanding.

Evidence grades apply to the exact claim/population/outcome below, not to an entire field. “B” means moderate-to-strong support for a qualified statement in the source's studied context; it does not mean an app-specific effect or a personal prediction.

## Activity boundary

The implemented S6 activity `activity.self-paced-word-recall` is a product demonstration, not a study result. It reports the number of distinct words from the displayed list entered in that one round. It has no normative sample, validated interpretation, delayed follow-up, or efficacy result. Its `efficacyEvidenceIds` is empty in the graph: the literature above offers background context but does not validate this product-specific task. See `limit.demo-is-not-the-reviewed-intervention`, `limit.demo-has-no-delayed-follow-up`, and `limit.demo-is-not-an-assessment`.

## Approved starting copy for S6

- “Working memory helps keep some information available while you use it. What can be held depends on the task and on how information is grouped.”
- “Try one short, self-paced recall demonstration. It reports what you entered in this round; it is not a test of your overall memory, working-memory capacity, intelligence, attention, or health.”
- “Retrieval practice can support learning in many studied educational settings. This one-round activity cannot tell you whether you personally will remember something later; that would require a delayed follow-up.”
- “Improvement on one practice task does not, by itself, show broad improvement in intelligence or everyday functioning.”

## Do not publish without new evidence and review

- A fixed capacity number presented as a universal limit or individualized result.
- “This exercise increases IQ,” “makes you smarter,” “rewires your brain,” or equivalent.
- Claims that the one-round demo shows durable learning, generalizes to school/work/life, or improves mental/physical health.
- Percentiles, rankings, brain age, diagnosis, or predictions based on an unvalidated task.
- An edge or diagram arrow that turns association/relationship into causation.

## Review trail and limitations

On 2026-10-04, the assigned evidence-review pass opened the Cowan and Melby-Lervåg full texts, the Agarwal and Dunlosky publisher pages, and the Roediger–Karpicke institutional abstract. Bibliographic identifiers/DOIs and the quoted abstract-level results were cross-checked against those records. The evidence review was completed by one agent in a separate role pass, not by an external psychologist or systematic-review team; no independent scientific review or new exhaustive literature search was performed. This is enough to scope a cautious prototype, not to certify clinical statements or a public evidence library. Recheck sources and current evidence when copy, audience, intervention, or intended use changes.
