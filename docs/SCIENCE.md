# Scientific integrity standard

This is a product-development standard, not a completed scientific review. Domain-specific evidence must be researched and cited before relevant claims or interventions are presented to users.

## Evidence workflow

For each user-facing scientific claim or recommended intervention:

1. Write the **exact claim**, target population, outcome, duration, and intended context.
2. Search authoritative, current sources: systematic reviews/meta-analyses, randomized trials, consensus statements, professional guidelines, and high-quality public-health or university sources. Use primary studies when they clarify mechanisms or limitations.
3. Verify the original source, date, population, methods, outcome, and limitations. A search snippet or another AI's bibliography is not verification.
4. Record the source and what *specific part* of the claim it supports in `docs/EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md` (to be created at Stage `S1`). Include a stable link/DOI where available.
5. Have a second evidence-focused reviewer check the claim/source fit and wording before publication.
6. Revisit claims when scope, evidence, or product copy changes.

Never fabricate, infer, or copy citations without opening and checking the source. If the exact claim cannot be verified, remove it or label it as uncertain and narrow it.

## Separate outcome levels

Always distinguish:

- **Task-specific practice:** better performance on the activity practiced.
- **Near transfer:** change on a similar task or related ability.
- **Far transfer:** change on broader, less similar abilities.
- **Real-world function:** change in study, work, daily life, or meaningful participation.
- **Health/clinical outcome:** a validated change in health status or disease risk.

These outcomes are not interchangeable. Also distinguish association from causation, mechanistic plausibility from tested effects, statistical significance from practical/clinical significance, and short-term practice effects from durable learning.

## Evidence labels

Use the project’s proposed A–E scale only as a compact summary; explain the reasoning and do not imply that a grade guarantees an effect for an individual.

- **A — Strong:** multiple high-quality and reasonably consistent sources support the defined outcome/population.
- **B — Moderate:** meaningful evidence exists, with important limitations or inconsistency.
- **C — Preliminary:** promising or narrow evidence; confidence is limited.
- **D — Weak/uncertain:** sparse, inconsistent, indirect, or methodologically weak evidence.
- **E — Unsupported/contradicted:** the claim is not supported as phrased or is contradicted by the best available evidence.

Rate a *specific claim/outcome/population*, not a whole topic such as “memory,” “exercise,” or an activity as if all uses were equivalent. When the evidence is mixed, show that rather than forcing false precision.

## User-facing language

- Say what an activity practices and what it measures (for example, accuracy or response time).
- Call short activities **educational demonstrations** or **practice**, not validated assessment, unless validation is established and documented.
- Describe change only at the level the evidence supports; do not extrapolate one game's score to intelligence, mental health, or daily functioning.
- Identify uncertainty, population limits, expected variability, and practice effects.
- Never use “rewires your brain,” “boosts IQ,” “prevents dementia,” or similar promotional shortcuts without precise, appropriate evidence and qualification.
- Use compassionate, non-alarmist language. Do not ridicule people for believing popular myths.

## Clinical and safety boundary

The platform provides education and self-development—not diagnosis or treatment. It must not diagnose mental, neurological, or physical conditions, prescribe or advise changing medication, or interpret game performance as evidence of a disorder. Where appropriate, encourage users to consult a qualified professional, especially for persistent, worsening, sudden, or concerning symptoms. Exercise, sleep, nutrition, stress, and supplement guidance must account for individual risk and avoid unsafe or categorical instructions.

## Minimum evidence record (Stage S1)

The evidence ledger should record at least: claim ID and exact wording; topic; population; outcome; evidence type; source/citation; what the source supports; limitations; transfer level; proposed label; review date; reviewer; and product locations using the claim. A claim without a verified source stays unpublished.
